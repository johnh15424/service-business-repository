FOR PET SITTING BUSINESSES

Overnight Pet Sitting Cost: Pricing Capacity, Time and Care

An overnight stay is not simply eight or ten billable labour hours. It occupies business capacity, constrains location and often includes evening and morning care tasks. Build the price from the actual commitment and opportunity cost, then compare it with realistic monthly capacity.

Overnight pricing is a capacity problem

A sitter may be able to complete daytime visits around an overnight booking, but the booking still controls where the sitter ends the day, starts the next morning and spends the night. The price should reflect that constraint as well as the active care time.

Main cost drivers

Active care time

Evening feeding, walks, medication, cleaning, morning routines and owner updates are active service tasks and should be estimated explicitly.

Location and travel

The overnight location can affect the next day's route. Include travel and any extra dead mileage created by the stay.

Number and needs of pets

Multiple animals, medication, behavioural needs and complex routines change the time and responsibility involved.

Opportunity cost

A low overnight price can block higher-value work if the booking prevents other evening, early-morning or distant jobs.

Overhead and payment costs

Insurance, scheduling, phone, accounting, marketing and payment fees still need recovery.

Build an overnight price

  1. Define arrival, departure and included care tasks.
  2. Estimate active labour during the stay.
  3. Add travel and route impact.
  4. Add direct consumables or special-care costs.
  5. Allocate overhead across realistic monthly overnight capacity.
  6. Consider the work the booking prevents the sitter from accepting.
  7. Add payment fees and apply target retained margin.
  8. Add tax separately where required.

Common overnight pricing mistakes

  • Multiplying every overnight clock hour by an hourly rate.
  • Ignoring the morning and evening care workload.
  • Ignoring the effect on the next day's route.
  • Charging the same for routine and high-needs pets.
  • Failing to measure monthly capacity before setting discounts.
