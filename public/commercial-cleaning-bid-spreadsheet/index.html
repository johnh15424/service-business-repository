FOR COMMERCIAL CLEANING CONTRACTORS

Commercial cleaning bid spreadsheet: cost the contract, not just the floor area

A useful commercial cleaning estimating spreadsheet connects the site specification to paid cleaner-hours, monthly contract costs and the margin left after fees and reserves. A single price per square metre cannot capture the work required by washrooms, kitchens, security procedures or periodic floor care.

Start with a zone-by-zone cleaning schedule

Record each cleaning zone, the area or task quantity, the expected output per paid cleaner-hour and how often it must be cleaned. Offices, washrooms, reception areas, kitchens and stairs have different productivity. For tasks such as waste collection, add fixed minutes per visit rather than dividing a meaningless floor area by a generic rate.

Weekly cleaner-hours = sum of (hours per task visit × weekly task frequency). Convert to a calendar-month average using 52 ÷ 12, not four weeks. If several zones are cleaned during the same visit, do not count each zone as a separate customer visit when pricing travel or consumables.

Cost paid time, including travel and cover

Calculate the fully burdened hourly employment cost, including the applicable employer contributions, paid leave and other staffing costs. Add paid travel, setup, supervision and security procedures. A two-person team working for three hours creates six cleaner-hours, even though the site is occupied for only three clock hours.

For a recurring contract, check whether the proposed cleaning window is realistic and whether holidays, absences or access restrictions require cover. Record these assumptions with the tender.

Separate routine supplies from periodic work

Cleaning chemicals, liners, cloths, PPE where required, machine servicing and replacement equipment should be costed using actual usage and supplier prices. Periodic carpet extraction, hard-floor maintenance or high-level work may happen monthly or quarterly, so convert those costs to a monthly provision rather than omitting them from the routine bid.

Include vehicle running costs, keyholding, waste disposal, insurance, account management, estimating and other contract overhead. Do not double count overhead that is already included in an hourly loaded cost.

Calculate the monthly selling price and retained margin

Once the monthly cost base is known, calculate the pre-tax contract price required to leave the chosen retained margin after selling-price-linked charges.

Monthly bid = monthly cost base ÷ (1 − margin rate − reserve rate − percentage fee rate). Use decimal rates in the calculation and ensure their sum is below 100%. Fixed payment fees need separate treatment. Apply the business's minimum contract value and tax treatment afterwards.

For illustration only, a monthly cost base of €5,000, a 28% target margin, a 5% reserve and a 1.5% payment fee imply €5,000 ÷ 0.655 = €7,633.59 before tax. This is not a market rate or a recommendation for any particular premises.

Test the bid against contract terms

Compare the result as a monthly price, an annual contract value, a price per routine visit and contribution per paid cleaner-hour. Check payment timing, consumable responsibility, inflation or wage review clauses, holiday cover, access, cancellations, quality audits and the cost of contract mobilisation.

A low monthly price may still be viable if the site sits on an efficient route and the schedule is stable. A higher price may be necessary for fragmented shifts, strict access windows or specialist work. Document the reasons rather than copying another contractor's price list.
