Dog Walking Cost: How to Set a Sustainable Service Price

Price the complete client visit and the route around it, not only the advertised walk duration. Travel, collection, handover, waste supplies, insurance, scheduling and cancellations all affect how many paid walks fit into a working day.

What drives dog walking cost?

Paid service time

Include the booked walk plus collection, handover, locking up and owner updates where these are part of the service.

Travel and route density

The usable working day is limited by movement between clients. A tightly grouped route can support more paid walks than a scattered diary.

Solo versus group walks

Group walks can improve revenue per hour, but collection, transport, matching dogs and handling complexity can add time and risk.

Insurance and equipment

Leads, waste bags, towels, cleaning supplies, vehicle setup and insurance belong in the cost model.

Cancellations and capacity

Allocate overhead across realistic paid walks rather than assuming every diary slot will sell every week.

A practical pricing model

  1. Define the walk type and service scope.
  2. Estimate total paid time including collection and handover.
  3. Add average route travel and vehicle cost.
  4. Add equipment, waste bags and other direct costs.
  5. Allocate overhead across realistic monthly paid walks.
  6. Allow for payment fees and predictable leakage.
  7. Apply target retained margin.
  8. Check short visits against a minimum service charge.

Common mistakes

  • Treating travel between dogs as unpaid personal time.
  • Using the walk duration as the only labour input.
  • Assuming group walks are automatically more profitable.
  • Ignoring cancellations when allocating overhead.
  • Copying local rates without comparing route density and service scope.

Measure the whole working day

Track the start and finish time of the route, not only the time spent walking. That gives a clearer view of effective revenue per working hour and shows which areas or service types consume too much travel.
