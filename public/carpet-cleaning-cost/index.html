FOR CARPET CLEANING BUSINESSES

How to price carpet cleaning from labour, treatment and machine cost

This guide is for carpet-cleaning businesses setting profitable prices, not householders comparing local quotes. Room count can help customers understand the offer, but the business should price from setup, labour, soil level, chemicals, machine use, travel and overhead.

Room count is convenient, but workload varies

A small heavily soiled room with spot treatment and furniture handling can take longer than a larger open room. Stairs, landings, tight access and long hose runs also change the job. If the business sells “per room” packages, define what a standard room includes and how oversized or unusually difficult areas are handled.

Estimate the full cleaning process

Count setup, inspection, vacuuming where included, pre-spray, agitation, dwell time that requires attendance, extraction, spotting, grooming, pack-down and customer handover. Travel should be included where staff are paid for it.

Multiply the resulting person-hours by the loaded labour cost. Owner-operated businesses should include a realistic value for owner time rather than treating it as free.

Chemicals and specialist treatments need recovery

Pre-spray, rinse agents, spotters, odour treatments, protector and specialist chemistry vary by job. Heavy soil, pet contamination and stain treatment can increase both material usage and labour. Price these directly when they are predictable, and define optional treatments separately where useful.

Machine economics matter

Portable extractors, truck mounts, vacuums, rotary machines, hoses, pumps and heaters need maintenance and replacement. Fuel, electricity and water can also be job-level costs. Build an allowance from actual service intervals and annual replacement spend instead of assuming the machine cost disappeared when it was purchased.

Minimum charges protect setup-heavy jobs

A single small room still requires travel, unloading, hose setup, cleaning and pack-down. A minimum charge prevents low-volume jobs from using a disproportionate amount of capacity without recovering the fixed visit cost.

A practical carpet-cleaning pricing sequence

  1. Define rooms, area, stairs and included treatments.
  2. Assess soil level, access and furniture handling.
  3. Estimate total paid labour time.
  4. Add chemicals, spot treatment and protector where included.
  5. Add machine wear, utilities and vehicle cost.
  6. Allocate monthly overhead across realistic jobs.
  7. Add fees and contingency where relevant.
  8. Apply the retained margin target and minimum charge.
  9. Add tax only where required.

Per-room versus per-area pricing

Per-room pricing is simple for customers. Area pricing can be more precise on open-plan or commercial work. Either can work if the business checks the underlying labour and treatment cost. The customer-facing unit is less important than the internal costing model.

Common carpet-cleaning pricing mistakes

  • treating every room as the same size and soil level
  • ignoring setup and pack-down time
  • including specialist spotting without allowance
  • forgetting machine service and hose wear
  • absorbing stairs and awkward access
  • pricing from competitor menus instead of actual costs
