FOR DOG WALKING BUSINESSES

Dog Walking Cost per Hour: Turn Route Economics into an Hourly Price

A nominal one-hour walk can consume more than one hour of business capacity once collection, travel and handover are included. Work out the cost of a realistic working hour first, then convert that requirement into per-walk prices.

Start with productive capacity

Do not divide monthly overhead by every hour on the calendar. Use the number of paid service hours the business can realistically sell after allowing for travel, admin, cancellations and gaps between clients.

What belongs in the hourly requirement?

Labour

Include the sitter or walker's true labour cost for both service time and paid movement between jobs.

Route travel

Vehicle cost and drive time should be reflected even if the customer sees one simple walk price.

Overhead

Insurance, software, phone, accounting, marketing and scheduling costs need to be spread across realistic paid capacity.

Payment fees and reserve

Card charges, no-shows and other predictable leakage reduce the amount the business retains from the headline price.

Turn an hourly requirement into walk prices

  1. Calculate the loaded labour cost per working hour.
  2. Add the average travel and vehicle cost per booking.
  3. Allocate monthly overhead across realistic paid walks.
  4. Add direct equipment or consumable cost.
  5. Apply payment fees and a measured contingency.
  6. Apply the target retained margin.
  7. Convert the result into 30-minute, 60-minute or group-walk prices while preserving the economics.

Why a simple hourly rate can mislead

If a one-hour walk requires 20 minutes of travel and handover, the business may consume 80 minutes of capacity to sell 60 minutes. A rate that only covers the advertised walk time understates the real labour commitment.
