DOG GROOMING COST BY SIZE

How much should dog grooming cost by dog size?

Dog grooming cost by dog size depends on coat, condition, service time and handling, not weight alone. Use a practical pricing framework and free calculator.

What changes the cost?

Size bands can be useful for quoting, but they should not replace time and cost. A small dog with a dense or matted coat can take longer than a larger short-coated dog. Build the quote around the actual appointment, then use size as one input rather than the entire pricing method.

  • coat type and density
  • coat condition and matting
  • service type and finishing work
  • handling or behavioural time
  • products, utilities and equipment wear
  • business overhead and payment fees

Build the price from the job, not a copied rate

A useful way to price is to estimate total paid minutes, multiply by the loaded labour rate, add products and equipment wear, allocate overhead, then apply the target margin. The calculator handles that structure while keeping every assumption editable.

Does a larger dog always cost more to groom?

Not necessarily. Size affects time and product use, but coat type, condition, service scope and handling can matter just as much.

Should grooming prices be based on breed or size?

Breed and size can help set an initial expectation, but the final price should reflect the real time and cost of the appointment.

How can a groomer make pricing more consistent?

Use the same costing method for every appointment, then adjust only the inputs that genuinely change, such as time, products and coat condition.
