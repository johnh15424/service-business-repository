FOR GUTTER CLEANING BUSINESSES

How to price gutter cleaning from height, access and real visit time

This guide is for gutter-cleaning businesses setting profitable prices, not homeowners comparing local quotes. Gutter length matters, but height, access, blockage, setup, disposal and travel can have a larger effect on the real cost of the visit.

Price the complete visit, not just the gutter length

A straightforward single-storey run with clear access can be completed very differently from a tall property with extensions, conservatories, narrow side passages or difficult corners. If the quote is based only on metres or feet, access-heavy work can quickly become unprofitable.

Estimate the paid time from arrival through setup, cleaning, downpipe checks, debris handling, pack-down and customer handover.

Height and access affect productivity

Ladder work, pole systems and gutter vacuums each have different setup and production profiles. The chosen method should suit the property, equipment and applicable safety requirements. Additional storeys, awkward rooflines and obstacles usually increase time even when the gutter length is unchanged.

Blocked downpipes should be defined in the scope

Removing loose gutter debris is different from diagnosing or clearing a blocked downpipe. If downpipe clearance is included, estimate the likely extra labour and equipment. If it is not included, state that clearly so a routine clean does not turn into open-ended drainage work.

Debris volume and disposal create cost

Wet leaves, moss and compacted material take longer to remove and can be heavier to transport. Some properties have suitable disposal arrangements while others require the contractor to remove the debris. Include bagging, loading and disposal time where the business is responsible for it.

Travel and minimum charges protect small jobs

A short gutter run can still require booking, driving, parking, equipment setup and invoicing. A minimum charge helps recover those fixed visit costs. Dense routes may support a lower effective minimum than isolated jobs because travel per customer is reduced.

A practical gutter-cleaning pricing sequence

  1. Estimate gutter length and number of elevations.
  2. Assess height, access and obstacles.
  3. Define whether downpipes and disposal are included.
  4. Estimate setup, cleaning and pack-down crew-hours.
  5. Add equipment wear, consumables and disposal cost.
  6. Add travel and vehicle cost.
  7. Allocate business overhead.
  8. Apply fees, contingency, minimum charge and retained margin.
  9. Add tax only where required.

Per-metre versus per-property pricing

Either model can work. Per-metre pricing is useful when access conditions are comparable. Per-property pricing is simpler for customers but should still be built from an internal estimate of length, height and time. For complex properties, use separate adjustments rather than forcing every job into one unit rate.

Common gutter-cleaning pricing mistakes

  • using gutter length without considering height
  • assuming all downpipes are part of a routine clean
  • forgetting setup and pack-down time
  • absorbing debris disposal
  • not recovering equipment maintenance
  • using the same minimum for nearby and remote work
