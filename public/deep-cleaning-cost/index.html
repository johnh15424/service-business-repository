FOR CLEANING BUSINESSES

How to price a deep clean without underestimating the labour

This guide is for cleaning businesses pricing deep-clean work, not homeowners comparing quotes. A deep clean should be costed from the expanded task list, expected person-hours, consumables and overhead rather than by adding an arbitrary percentage to a standard clean.

A deep clean is a different production job

Deep cleaning usually changes both the scope and the pace of work. Detailed kitchen degreasing, bathroom scale removal, skirting boards, door frames, reachable high dust, internal glass, cabinet fronts and other intensive tasks can add substantial labour even when the property size is unchanged.

Write the scope before pricing it. If customers can interpret “deep clean” differently from the business, margin problems and service disputes follow. Separate included tasks, optional extras and excluded tasks so the time estimate has a clear basis.

Price from person-hours, not a standard-clean multiplier

A percentage uplift can be useful as a sense check, but it should not replace costing. Estimate how many cleaners are required and how long each stage should take. Three cleaners for four hours is twelve person-hours. Add paid travel and any setup or laundry handling that creates additional labour.

Then multiply total person-hours by the loaded labour cost, including employer on-costs where relevant. Owner-operated businesses should still include a realistic labour cost for the owner's time.

Heavy soil changes more than labour

Deep cleaning can increase chemical consumption, cloth usage, laundry, scraper or pad wear and waste handling. Grease, limescale, pet contamination or neglected surfaces may require specialist products and dwell time. Cost these directly when they are predictable rather than relying on general overhead to absorb them.

First-time cleans need uncertainty protection

A recurring customer has a known property and known maintenance standard. A first-time deep clean has more uncertainty. Photographs, a walkthrough or a structured customer questionnaire can improve the estimate, but there may still be hidden workload.

Businesses can protect against that uncertainty with a defined scope, a sensible contingency allowance or a clear policy for work discovered outside the quoted scope. The goal is not to inflate every quote, but to avoid promising unlimited labour for a fixed price.

Fixed price or hourly pricing for deep cleans?

Fixed pricing can work well when the scope is clear and the business has reliable productivity data. Hourly or capped-hour pricing can be safer for heavily neglected or uncertain properties. Some businesses quote a fixed core scope and price exceptional tasks separately.

Whichever method the customer sees, the internal pricing model should still calculate labour, supplies, travel, overhead and margin.

Deep-clean pricing sequence

  1. Define the detailed task list and exclusions.
  2. Estimate person-hours by area or task group.
  3. Add loaded labour cost.
  4. Add extra chemicals, cloths, laundry and equipment wear.
  5. Add travel and vehicle cost where relevant.
  6. Allocate overhead across realistic paid jobs.
  7. Add fees and contingency where appropriate.
  8. Apply the target retained margin and minimum charge.
  9. Add tax only where required.

Common deep-clean pricing failures

  • adding a flat percentage without estimating extra hours
  • failing to define what “deep” includes
  • treating oven, fridge or cabinet interiors as automatically included
  • underestimating first-time kitchen and bathroom detail
  • forgetting extra laundry and consumable usage
  • quoting from floor area alone
  • not recording actual hours after the job

Use completed deep cleans to improve future quotes

Track estimated versus actual person-hours by property type and task mix. Over time, the business can build its own productivity benchmarks for kitchens, bathrooms, internal windows and other recurring components. Those records are far more useful than a generic market average because they reflect the company's actual team, process and service standard.
