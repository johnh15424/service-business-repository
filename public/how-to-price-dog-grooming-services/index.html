HOW TO PRICE DOG GROOMING SERVICES

Build the price from
the work underneath it.

A useful grooming price starts with the appointment you actually deliver, not a copied competitor number.

Build a grooming price ↗

1. Define the service clearly

Start with what the appointment includes. A bath and brush, full groom, deshedding treatment, puppy introduction, nail trim and hand stripping session do not use the same time, products or equipment.

Keep optional add-ons and exceptional work separate so the base service remains understandable.

2. Estimate the real appointment time

Use grooming time plus extra coat work, handling, setup and clean-up. For a team appointment, use person-minutes. A 60-minute appointment involving two groomers for the full hour represents two labour hours.

Review actual completed appointments periodically. If a service consistently takes longer than the menu assumption, the cost model is understated.

3. Calculate loaded labour cost

Use a realistic hourly cost for the groomer's time and add employer on-costs where relevant. Self-employed owner time should still be valued, otherwise the model can mistake unpaid labour for profit.

4. Add appointment-level costs

Include shampoo, conditioner, specialist products, blade/equipment wear, water, electricity and any other cost that increases when you perform another groom.

See the dog grooming business cost checklist for a fuller breakdown.

5. Allocate fixed overhead

Rent, insurance, software, phone, accounting and similar fixed costs need to be funded by the appointments you sell. A simple planning method is monthly fixed overhead divided by realistic paid appointments.

Allocated overhead = monthly fixed overhead ÷ expected paid appointments

6. Add mobile travel where relevant

Mobile businesses should account for both travel time and vehicle cost. Route density can change the economics of two otherwise identical grooms. See the mobile grooming pricing guide.

7. Include payment fees and a sensible minimum

Card and booking fees reduce what you retain. A minimum charge also protects short appointments where setup, cleaning, administration or travel create a fixed cost regardless of grooming time.

8. Choose a margin target, not a markup by accident

Margin and markup are different calculations. If your goal is a target margin, solve the price from cost rather than simply adding the same percentage to cost. See margin versus markup explained.

9. Apply tax after the pre-tax price

When tax applies, calculate the business price first and then add VAT, GST, HST or sales tax according to your actual obligations. Tax collected for a tax authority is not business profit.

10. Compare the result with the market intelligently

A cost-based calculation gives you an economic floor and target. Market research then tells you whether the offer, positioning or operating model needs adjustment. If your required price is far above local alternatives, inspect productivity, overhead, capacity and service design rather than deleting costs from the model.

Build one real appointment now

Use a recent dog you remember well and replace every example with your own figures. That gives you a more useful answer than any generic price table.
