FOR SOLAR PANEL CLEANING CONTRACTORS

Commercial Solar Panel Cleaning Cost: A Contractor Pricing Model

Commercial arrays can improve productivity, but they also introduce mobilisation, access, water-production, site-control and scheduling costs. Price the work around crew-hours and site requirements before converting the result into a rate per panel or per square metre.

Commercial pricing starts with mobilisation

Large sites often have fixed work before cleaning begins: induction, loading, access planning, hose setup, water preparation, isolating work areas and moving between array sections. These costs do not scale neatly with panel count, so a minimum mobilisation component can be necessary.

The main commercial cost drivers

Crew-hours

Use total person-hours, not elapsed site time. Two technicians working three hours is six labour hours before travel.

Array layout and access

Ground-mounted arrays, flat roofs, multiple buildings and long runs between sections can create very different productivity.

Purified water capacity

Estimate how much water the job needs, where it will be produced and whether production or transport limits daily output.

Equipment and replacement

Commercial work can accelerate wear on poles, brushes, pumps, filtration equipment, hoses and reels. Recover that use in the job economics.

Working windows and compliance

Restricted access times, escorts, inductions and customer paperwork can add paid time even when the cleaning process itself is straightforward.

Build the commercial price

  1. Estimate total crew-hours including travel, setup and pack-down.
  2. Add purified water, filters, resin and other direct consumables.
  3. Add vehicle and equipment-use costs.
  4. Add site-specific access or compliance costs.
  5. Allocate overhead across realistic commercial capacity.
  6. Include payment fees or contingency where appropriate.
  7. Apply target retained margin.
  8. Only then calculate a per-panel or area rate if the tender needs one.

When a unit rate helps

A per-panel or per-square-metre rate is useful for consistent tenders and repeat work, but it should be derived from a full job model. If mobilisation or access changes, the unit rate should change too.

Common commercial pricing mistakes

  • Assuming a bigger array always means lower cost per panel.
  • Ignoring induction and restricted-access time.
  • Failing to account for water-production limits.
  • Using one rate across sites with very different layouts.
  • Discounting repeat contracts before actual productivity is measured.
