FOR SOLAR PANEL CLEANING BUSINESSES

Solar Panel Cleaning Cost: How to Price the Job

Panel count is useful for describing scope, but a profitable quote still has to recover travel, setup, access, purified water, equipment use and overhead. Build the price from the work required rather than starting with a copied rate per panel.

What actually drives solar panel cleaning cost?

Paid labour and setup

Count loading, travel, setup, access preparation, cleaning, inspection and pack-down. A short cleaning pass can still be an expensive call-out if setup and travel are ignored.

Access and array layout

Single-storey arrays, roof-mounted panels, multiple roof faces, restricted gardens and commercial arrays have different labour profiles. Access time belongs in the estimate even if the panel count is identical.

Purified water and consumables

Resin, filters, purified water production, brushes, hoses and approved cleaning materials create direct or equipment-use costs. They should not disappear into an undefined overhead figure.

Equipment wear

Water-fed poles, pumps, filtration systems, reels, hoses and vehicle storage equipment have finite lives. A pricing model should gradually recover servicing and replacement.

Travel and route density

A cluster of nearby residential jobs can have very different economics from isolated rural call-outs. Route density changes the amount of paid work that fits into a day.

A practical pricing method

  1. Define the exact array and access scope.
  2. Estimate paid labour including setup, travel and pack-down.
  3. Add purified water, consumables and equipment-use costs.
  4. Add vehicle cost and any job-specific access expense.
  5. Allocate a realistic share of monthly overhead.
  6. Include payment fees and a measured contingency where appropriate.
  7. Apply the target retained margin to the complete pre-tax cost base.
  8. Check the result against the minimum charge needed for a call-out.
  9. Add tax separately only where the business is required to charge it.

Common mistakes that erode margin

  • Quoting only from panel count while ignoring access and setup.
  • Treating purified water production and filtration as free.
  • Using owner labour at zero cost.
  • Copying another contractor's rate without knowing their route density or equipment model.
  • Confusing markup with retained margin.

Use completed jobs to improve the model

Compare estimated labour, travel and water use with completed work. If difficult access, heavy soiling or rural travel repeatedly takes longer than expected, update the assumptions rather than hoping the margin absorbs the difference. If productivity improves, record that too.

Solar panel cleaning pricing questions

Should I charge per panel?

A per-panel figure can be useful for quoting consistency, but calculate the required job price first. Then divide that price by the panel count if a unit rate helps present or compare the quote.

Should travel be a separate line item?

Not necessarily. The customer does not need to see every internal cost, but the business still needs to recover paid travel time and vehicle cost somewhere in the price.

How often should prices be reviewed?

Review them when labour, water-production, equipment, insurance, payment fees or route productivity changes materially.
