FOR DOG GROOMING BUSINESSES

How to work out a sustainable dog grooming hourly rate

This guide is for groomers checking the economics behind their service prices, not pet owners comparing salons. An internal hourly rate should recover labour, consumables, equipment, premises, administration and target margin rather than being based on wage alone.

Why groomers need an internal hourly benchmark

Customers are usually quoted by service rather than by the clock, but an hourly benchmark is still one of the clearest ways to test profitability. If a full groom takes two hours and the service price only supports one profitable hour, the problem will eventually show up as low margin, rushed appointments or a diary that feels busy without producing enough profit.

The hourly figure should therefore be used behind the price list. It helps the business compare services, assess add-on work and identify appointments that consistently consume more time than their price allows.

Start with loaded labour cost

Employee wage is not always the full labour cost. Depending on the business and country, employer taxes, pension contributions, holiday cost or other payroll on-costs may need to be included. Owner-operated salons should also assign a realistic cost to the owner's working time. Treating owner labour as free can make a profitable-looking price unsustainable.

Add the grooming costs created by the appointment

Shampoo, conditioner, specialist products, ear cleaner, finishing sprays, bows, towels, water, electricity and laundry can all vary by service. Blade sharpening, clipper maintenance, dryer wear and table or bath replacement also need recovery over time.

Not every item needs to be measured to the cent on every dog. The business can use sensible per-appointment allowances based on actual usage and review them periodically.

Convert overhead into an hourly burden

Rent, insurance, booking software, phone, card terminal, marketing, accounting and admin exist even when no dog is on the table. A simple method is to allocate monthly overhead across realistic paid appointments or productive grooming hours.

Do not divide by every hour the salon is technically open. Use the hours or appointments that are realistically billable after cancellations, breaks, cleaning, admin and unused capacity.

Hourly cost is not the same as hourly selling price

The cost per productive hour tells you what the business spends to deliver the work. The selling rate must also allow for payment fees, contingency and profit. Margin and markup are different: adding 40% to cost does not create a 40% margin.

A useful internal model is to calculate the required pre-tax selling price for an appointment, then divide by the expected productive hours. That gives an effective hourly selling rate for comparison across services.

Use hourly economics behind fixed service prices

A groomer may publish prices for full grooms, bath and brush, de-shedding, puppy introductions or hand stripping. Those prices can still be built from time. Estimate the expected minutes, apply the internal labour and overhead model, add service-specific product costs and then set a customer-facing price.

This is especially useful for services that appear similar on the menu but use very different amounts of time or equipment.

How to calculate the rate step by step

  1. Calculate loaded labour cost per hour.
  2. Add an hourly or per-appointment share of overhead.
  3. Add average consumables, utilities and equipment wear.
  4. Allow for transaction fees and predictable leakage.
  5. Apply the target retained margin.
  6. Use the resulting hourly benchmark to test each service price.
  7. Compare expected minutes with actual appointment records and refine the model.

Common hourly-rate mistakes

  • using employee wage as the full business cost
  • ignoring cleanup and customer handover time
  • spreading overhead across unrealistic maximum capacity
  • failing to recover blade, clipper and dryer wear
  • treating all services as equally productive
  • copying another salon's hourly figure
