FOR COMMERCIAL CARPET CLEANERS

How to price commercial carpet cleaning from site productivity

This guide is for cleaning contractors pricing offices, hospitality, retail and other commercial sites, not facilities buyers comparing quotes. Commercial pricing should start with method, crew productivity, furniture density, access and direct treatment costs before a square-foot or square-metre rate is set.

Large area does not automatically mean simple work

Open carpet can be productive, but offices with desks, partitioning, cables, lifts, restricted access or occupied areas can slow the crew. Site induction, parking, key collection and after-hours access can also consume paid time that is not visible in the measured carpet area.

Estimate the whole visit from arrival to departure, then convert the finished job price into an area rate if the client expects one.

Cleaning method changes production speed

Hot-water extraction, encapsulation and other low-moisture methods have different labour, chemical, drying and machine requirements. The right method depends on carpet type, soil, service objective and site constraints. Do not use one productivity assumption across every method.

Where a site requires pre-vacuuming, spotting, agitation, extraction and grooming, include each stage. If furniture is moved, define what is included and what remains in place.

Commercial productivity should be measured, not guessed

Track actual square feet or square metres completed per crew-hour on representative jobs. Build separate benchmarks for open-plan space, dense office layouts, corridors, stairs and heavy-soil work. These internal productivity records are more valuable than a generic industry figure because they reflect the company's own equipment and team.

Site access belongs in the price

After-hours work, loading-bay restrictions, lift access, security sign-in and long hose runs can reduce productive cleaning time. If a site requires additional staff for security or coordination, that labour should be captured too.

Build the area rate from the site price

  1. Measure the carpet area and define the method.
  2. Estimate crew-hours from realistic productivity.
  3. Add setup, access and travel time.
  4. Add chemicals, spot treatment and machine wear.
  5. Add utilities, vehicle and parking costs where relevant.
  6. Allocate overhead and contract administration.
  7. Add fees and contingency where appropriate.
  8. Apply the retained margin target.
  9. Divide the required pre-tax price by area if a unit rate is needed.

Recurring contracts need performance reviews

Repeat sites may become faster as the crew learns the layout, but soil load, occupancy and service frequency can change. Compare quoted hours with actual completion time and chemical use. If the contract repeatedly overruns, update the assumptions rather than expecting the team to absorb the difference.

Common commercial carpet-pricing mistakes

  • quoting from area without checking furniture density
  • using one productivity rate for every cleaning method
  • ignoring induction and access time
  • including specialist spot treatment without allowance
  • failing to recover machine maintenance
  • using a tender unit rate copied from a different site
