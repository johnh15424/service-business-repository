Pool Cleaning Cost: How to Price a Service Visit

A routine pool price should start with technician time, route time, chemicals, testing supplies and equipment use. Then allocate overhead and apply the target margin. Recalibrate the model from actual visits because pool condition and chemical demand can change seasonally.

What drives pool cleaning cost?

Service labour

Count skimming, brushing, vacuuming, testing, dosing, filter tasks, setup and customer notes.

Travel and route planning

Pool service is route-based. Drive time between accounts affects the number of paid visits a technician can complete.

Chemicals and testing supplies

Chlorine, acid, salt, shock, clarifier, reagents and strips should be based on expected usage rather than treated as an unlimited flat overhead.

Pool condition

Routine maintenance, storm clean-up and green-pool recovery require very different time and chemical inputs.

Equipment wear

Poles, brushes, nets, vacuums, hoses, pumps and test equipment all have servicing and replacement costs.

A practical pricing model

  1. Define the service tasks included in the visit.
  2. Estimate technician time and average route time.
  3. Add chemicals and testing supplies.
  4. Add equipment-use and vehicle costs.
  5. Allocate monthly overhead across realistic paid visits.
  6. Include payment fees and contingency where appropriate.
  7. Apply target retained margin.
  8. Use a minimum visit charge if travel and setup dominate small jobs.
  9. Add tax separately where required.

Common mistakes

  • Using one visit price regardless of pool condition.
  • Ignoring route time and technician capacity.
  • Treating chemicals as a flat overhead regardless of usage.
  • Pricing green-pool recovery as routine maintenance.
  • Failing to review recurring accounts after cost changes.

Use service records to improve pricing

Track visit duration, chemical usage and repeat visits by account. If an account consistently consumes more technician time or chemicals than the service tier assumes, the price or scope needs review.
